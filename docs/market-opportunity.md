# GiwaPay market opportunity and pilot plan

Status: **evidence-backed reference market plus scenario assumptions; not a
revenue forecast**

Portable decision report:
[`docs/market-report/report.html`](market-report/report.html). The HTML is

## Executive summary

- **There is an observable cross-border sales-volume baseline.** Korea's
  Ministry of Data and Statistics reported KRW 3.0234 trillion in online
  overseas direct sales in 2025. Excluding duty-free sales leaves KRW 1.9621
  trillion in non-duty-free online overseas direct sales. The release does not
  report a count of eligible small merchants.
- **That figure is a reference market, not GiwaPay's TAM.** The share of
  reference sales value from merchants that can legally and operationally
  accept a GIWA-settled token, and the share of their eligible checkout value
  that GiwaPay could process, are not yet measured.
- **A narrow first-year scenario is more credible than a large headline.**
  Applying explicit eligible-sales-volume and GiwaPay-value-share assumptions
  produces KRW 98 million to KRW 3.92 billion of annual processed volume, with
  a KRW 981 million base case. These values are sensitivity cases, not targets.
- **The next decision is demand validation.** Fifteen merchant interviews,
  three written pilot interests, and two testnet integrations would replace the
  model's two weakest assumptions with observed evidence.

## Market boundary

The first wedge is:

- South Korean small merchants already selling physical or digital products to
  overseas customers;
- a direct settlement-token payment path, without relying on a production DEX;
- customers who already have a compatible EVM wallet and supported asset; and
- a testnet pilot before any production asset, price, or regulatory claim.

The measure is annual customer payment volume processed by GiwaPay, not company
valuation, merchant revenue, or total stablecoin supply.

## Sourced reference market

The Korea Ministry of Data and Statistics' 2025 fourth-quarter online shopping
release reports:

| Measure                      |        2024 |        2025 |
| ---------------------------- | ----------: | ----------: |
| Online overseas direct sales | KRW 2.5977T | KRW 3.0234T |
| Of which duty-free           | KRW 0.9494T | KRW 1.0613T |
| Excluding duty-free          | KRW 1.6483T | KRW 1.9621T |

The 2025 total increased by about 16.4% year over year. GiwaPay uses the
KRW 1.9621 trillion non-duty-free amount only as the broadest observable
sales-volume baseline.

Primary source:
[Korea Ministry of Data and Statistics, 2025 Q4 online overseas direct sales and purchases](https://mods.go.kr/boardDownload.es?bid=241&list_no=443337&seq=1).

## Scenario model

Formula:

```text
processed volume
  = non-duty-free overseas direct sales
  × share of sales volume from operationally eligible merchants
  × GiwaPay share of eligible checkout value
```

An illustrative 0.5% platform fee is shown only to make the model inspectable.
It matches the repository's current default fee configuration, but pricing is
not validated and the merchant's signed settlement amount is never reduced.

| Scenario | Eligible sales-volume share | GiwaPay share of eligible checkout value | Annual processed volume | Illustrative 0.5% fee |
| -------- | --------------------------: | ---------------------------------------: | ----------------------: | --------------------: |
| Low      |                        0.1% |                                       5% |               KRW 98.1M |             KRW 0.49M |
| Base     |                        0.5% |                                      10% |              KRW 981.1M |             KRW 4.91M |
| High     |                        1.0% |                                      20% |               KRW 3.92B |            KRW 19.62M |

The model is most sensitive to two unknowns:

1. the share of cross-border sales volume generated by merchants able and
   willing to settle in a supported token; and
2. the share of those merchants' eligible checkout value that GiwaPay can
   process.

Because both are assumptions, none of the scenarios should appear as committed
GMV or revenue in an application.

## Beachhead hypothesis

Prioritize merchants with:

- cross-border customers but a small operations team;
- digital goods, creator products, software, memberships, or shippable D2C
  goods with low return complexity;
- an existing community that already uses wallets or digital assets;
- willingness to choose one direct settlement asset for the pilot; and
- a server-side integration point for payment status and webhooks.

Avoid starting with regulated financial products, anonymous high-risk goods,
chargeback-heavy categories, fiat conversion promises, or merchants that
require a production KRW stablecoin on day one.

## Four-week validation plan

### Week 1 — problem interviews

Recruit 15 merchants through founder communities, cross-border seller groups,
creator communities, and existing professional contacts.

Ask:

1. How do overseas customers pay today?
2. Where do fees, settlement delay, failed payment reconciliation, or currency
   mismatch create manual work?
3. Which settlement asset, if any, can the business legally and operationally
   hold?
4. What evidence must finance or support teams see before treating an order as
   paid?
5. How many customers already use a compatible wallet?
6. What would prevent a testnet pilot?

Do not lead with crypto or ask whether the merchant “likes” the product.

### Week 2 — written pilot interest

Ask interested merchants to confirm a non-binding statement:

> We are willing to evaluate a GiwaPay testnet checkout for one clearly defined
> product or customer cohort. This is not a commitment to process real funds or
> to use an unreleased GIWA, wallet, stablecoin, exchange, or fiat integration.
> We will provide feedback on setup time, customer comprehension, and receipt
> reconciliation.

Target: three signed or email-confirmed statements. Until they exist, do not
claim merchant commitments.

### Weeks 3–4 — two testnet integrations

Use the direct settlement-token path first. Measure:

- time from credentials to first test PaymentIntent;
- time from checkout open to wallet submission;
- share of submitted transactions that reach a canonical verified receipt;
- receipt and webhook latency;
- failure reason by stage;
- customer understanding of exact settlement versus maximum input; and
- merchant support or reconciliation steps per test order.

## Pilot success gate

Proceed to a production-asset and liquidity review only if:

- at least three merchants provide written pilot interest;
- two merchants complete a testnet integration;
- each runs at least 20 disposable test payments;
- no frontend-only or transaction-hash-only state is recorded as success;
- at least 95% of successful onchain payments produce the expected canonical
  receipt within the agreed test window; and
- every remaining failure has a classified, recoverable stage.

These thresholds are proposed validation gates, not current results.

## Evidence still missing

- independently documented merchant interviews;
- written pilot interests or LOIs;
- wallet-ready customer share in the target segment;
- a legally reviewed production settlement asset;
- production DEX liquidity and routing costs;
- customer acquisition cost, support load, and observed checkout conversion;
- actual willingness to pay.

The market case should be updated after each interview batch rather than
turning the current scenario model into a fixed forecast.
